ACT ONE

EXT. F-ROAD — END OF THE TRACK — SATURDAY, 09:00

Two rented super-jeeps, parked where the gravel gives up. No other cars. No signal bars on anyone's phone, though nobody has checked yet.

Strapped to the roof of the first jeep: the Costco trolley, empty, gleaming, entirely impractical.

Sigríður stands at the boot of the second jeep with a printed list, counting water bottles the way she used to count girls.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

(moving down the row)

Twelve.

MARÍA

Thirteen.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

I'm getting twelve.

MARÍA

It's been thirteen since the shop.

María opens the boot fully. Inside: an enormous gas canister — the kind meant for heating an entire summer house — and, wedged in beside it, a full cast-iron fire pit.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

María. What is that.

MARÍA

Atmosphere.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

We're carrying it.

MARÍA

It was on the list.

HULDA

You wrote the list.

MARÍA

(already walking, strap in hand)

Everyone take a corner.

Hulda and Gyða each take a corner of the canister. They lift it twenty centimetres. They set it back down.

Silence.

Rakel unstraps the trolley from the roof. Sets it down.

Seven women look at the trolley.

The trolley looks back.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

(to Rakel, re: the trolley)

Does Davíð know you took his trolley.

RAKEL

Davíð's never met the trolley.

RAKEL (CONT'D)

It's got wheels.

Silence.

They load the canister into the trolley.

Rakel pulls. The wheels sink instantly into the moss. She pulls harder. The trolley inches forward.

RAKEL (CONT'D)

It rolls.

SÚSANNA

It's not rolling, Rakel.

RAKEL

It's rolling in spirit.

Stefanía stands apart from all of this, hands on hips, scanning the treeline out of long habit. There is nothing to scan for. She does it anyway.

UNNUR

(noticing)

You can stop doing that now.

STEFANÍA

(instantly normal)

Doing what.

EXT. HIGHLAND PATH — MOVING — 09:40

The eight of them, strung out in a line. The trolley drags behind Rakel with a sound like a small avalanche. The gas canister rides on María's back like a scuba tank.

Hulda is out in front, moving with total confidence, no map in hand.

UNNUR

Do you actually know where we're going.

HULDA

Every bush out here. Every single one.

UNNUR

You got lost in Kringlan for forty minutes last month.

HULDA

Different terrain.

They walk on. Gyða falls into step beside a low ridge of black rock, moss-covered, ancient-looking.

GYÐA

(to Unnur, mock-careful)

Careful here. This is exactly your kind of neighbourhood.

UNNUR

What does that mean.

GYÐA

The Frenchman was right about you.

UNNUR

(flat)

I never confirmed that story.

STEFANÍA

Nobody had to confirm it. We were all there.

Unnur says nothing. She does, however, glance at the rock formation for slightly longer than necessary before walking on.

ACT TWO

EXT. HIGHLAND PATH — A DIFFERENT SPOT THAT LOOKS EXACTLY THE SAME — 11:15

The group has stopped. Eight identical backpacks. Nobody says the word "lost."

Hulda crouches. Puts one flat hand on the moss. Closes her eyes.

HULDA

I know what we need to do.

Beat.

HULDA (CONT'D)

Follow me.

She's already walking. Nobody moves for a second. Then everybody follows, because there is no alternative plan.

Súsanna falls in at the back, turns to the group, entirely serious.

SÚSANNA

One rule, out here. Nobody plays Phoebe.

GYÐA

Who's playing Phoebe.

SÚSANNA

Exactly. Keep it that way.

RAKEL

(to the trolley, not the group)

We're nearly there.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

Are we.

RAKEL

I'm telling it, not you.

EXT. THE HIGHLANDS — 12:50

The fog is thicker. They've stopped again.

Hulda stands alone, ten metres from the group.

Stefanía comes over to her.

A long silence.

STEFANÍA

Do you know where we are.

Silence.

HULDA

No.

STEFANÍA

Okay.

HULDA

I know where we were.

STEFANÍA

That'll do.

Stefanía takes out her phone. No signal. She puts it back in her pocket and takes out a compass.

Hulda looks at the compass.

HULDA

Where did that come from.

STEFANÍA

Work.

HULDA

You brought a compass.

STEFANÍA

I brought three.

Silence.

HULDA

Why didn't you say anything.

Stefanía hands her the compass.

STEFANÍA

You were leading.

Hulda takes it. She looks at it for four seconds. Then she points — in exactly the direction she was already going.

HULDA

There.

STEFANÍA

I know.

EXT. THE HIGHLANDS — 14:30

The fog opens.

And there's the valley. Green and red and impossible, like someone painted a mountain wrong on purpose.

Eight women stand and say nothing.

Sigríður takes out her phone and takes a photo. No signal. She puts it away.

Hulda sits on a rock and looks.

HULDA

Told you.

Nobody answers her.

She's right, and they all know it.

EXT. CAMPSITE — VALLEY FLOOR — 15:30

They arrive. A flat stretch of ground by a river, another campsite visible in the distance — a proper one, with actual camping equipment.

Rakel rolls the trolley the last ten metres and parks it. The canister is intact. The trolley is not.

María sets down the fire pit and starts assembling it immediately, methodically, from a laminated sheet she pulled out of an inside pocket.

HULDA

Is that laminated.

MARÍA

Otherwise it gets wet.

Nobody argues with that.

EXT. THE CAMPSITE — 15:40

Súsanna opens her backpack.

She takes out a travel washing machine.

Seven women look at her.

Súsanna looks back.

Nobody says a word.

She sets it down by her tent, fetches water from the river, and puts her socks in it.

Unnur walks past with tent poles.

She says nothing.

Súsanna threading a clothesline between two rocks. Unnur, nearby, unprompted:

UNNUR

Little bit higher or it'll—

SÚSANNA

—drag in the mud, I know.

They both keep working. Neither registers that they spoke over each other.

Gyða, watching from her tent, says nothing. Files it away.

ACT THREE

EXT. CAMPSITE — EVENING — 20:50

The fire pit is lit. It is, by any reasonable standard, too large. The flame is visible from some distance. The eight of them sit around it in camping chairs, perfectly content.

A STRANGER (30s, sensible outdoor jacket) appears at the edge of the light, having walked over from the other campsite.

STRANGER

Sorry — is everything alright over here?

MARÍA

Very.

STRANGER

We thought it might be a rescue flare.

MARÍA

It's atmosphere.

The stranger looks at the fire a moment longer than is comfortable, then retreats the way he came.

Beat.

GYÐA

(watching him go)

They always do that around you.

UNNUR

He was worried about a wildfire.

GYÐA

That's what they all say.

EXT. CAMPSITE — EVENING — 21:20

The fire lower now. María lifts her phone. No signal. Sets it on her knee.

Forty seconds later she lifts it again.

HULDA

There's no signal, Maja.

MARÍA

I know.

HULDA

You've checked four times.

MARÍA

Five.

Súsanna doesn't look up from the washing line.

SÚSANNA

She's on Tinder.

MARÍA

I'm not on Tinder.

SÚSANNA

You were on Tinder in Sweden.

MARÍA

That was a last chance.

Beat.

GYÐA

(sincerely practical)

Are there any women on Tinder up here?

HULDA

There's one woman every twenty kilometres and she's on shift.

GYÐA

Then it's not much of a pool.

MARÍA

I'm not on Tinder.

Nobody follows it up. Sigríður hands her a coffee. The conversation moves on to whether the river is safe to drink from.

Stefanía comes to the fire with three compasses in her hand and takes the free chair — the one next to María.

María takes a Nocco out of her own rucksack and hands it to her before she's asked.

Then she stands up.

STEFANÍA

Are you off?

MARÍA

I'll get more coffee.

There is a full flask by her foot.

She takes it with her.

EXT. CAMPSITE — NIGHT — 22:40

The fire is dying down. Someone should organise the tents, the watch order, who's up first for coffee. Nobody has asked anyone to do this.

Stefanía stands, brushes herself off, and does it anyway.

STEFANÍA

Right. Tents by the rock. Dinner's done, so kettle on at seven. Boots off before anyone comes near my sleeping bag.

A short silence. Then everyone starts moving, because it is, once again, exactly the right call.

HULDA

(to María, quietly)

She's still doing it.

MARÍA

We all are.

Sigríður stands up and walks a circle around the group.

She's counting.

MARÍA

Sigga.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

Mm.

MARÍA

It's just us.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

I know.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS (CONT'D)

Eight.

Wide shot: the fire, the river, the trolley leaning at an angle no trolley should stand at, a travel washing machine full of socks, and eight women who came here to switch off and brought the whole operation with them instead.

ACT ONE

EXT. CAMPSITE — STRIKING CAMP — 08:15

Efficient chaos. Tents coming down fast. Sigríður moving through with yesterday's list, now amended in pen.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

(counting)

Eight.

MARÍA

Still thirteen bags.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

I wasn't counting bags.

Rakel is already loading the trolley — sleeping bags stacked at an angle no engineer would sign off on.

HULDA

(watching)

It's going to tip on the first rock.

RAKEL

It's not going near rocks. It's going near me.

Gyða zips past with two bags, glances at the sky.

EXT. HIGHLAND PATH — DESCENDING — 09:50

The eight of them moving faster than yesterday, gear slightly heavier for being wet. Wind picking up. The gas canister, empty now, rides on María's back with a hollow clank on every second step.

Stefanía out in front today — not because anyone asked her to be.

STEFANÍA

(over her shoulder)

We keep together. Nobody stops for photos.

SÚSANNA

(already taking one)

Copy.

They reach a ridge. Below them: the river. Wider than yesterday. Faster. A brown, moving thing that was, twenty-four hours ago, a gentle glassy crossing four inches deep.

A long silence.

HULDA

That's not the same river.

STEFANÍA

It's the same river.

HULDA

It's not behaving like the same river.

ACT TWO

EXT. RIVERBANK — 10:20

The group strung along the bank, sizing it up.

Unnur crouches at the water's edge, watching the current a moment longer than the others.

UNNUR

It's fastest on the left third. Cross right of the big rock, not left.

STEFANÍA

(glancing over)

How do you know that.

UNNUR

I used to read rivers for a living. Different life.

Nobody asks which life. She doesn't offer it.

Stefanía already unclipping her pack straps — the ones you undo before a crossing, in case you go in.

Nobody taught her that. She just does it.

STEFANÍA

Loose straps. If you go down, you get the bag off you, not the other way round.

Sigríður looks at her own straps like they've betrayed her.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

When did you learn that.

STEFANÍA

I didn't. It's just correct.

Rakel stands at the water's edge with the trolley. Stefanía looks at it. Looks at the river. Looks at Rakel.

STEFANÍA (CONT'D)

(carefully, the way you'd address a hostage negotiation)

Rakel. I think the trolley should wait this one out.

RAKEL

It's coming.

STEFANÍA

It's four wheels and a basket. It has no opinion on this.

RAKEL

It has an opinion.

Beat. Nobody argues further. This was always how this was going to go.

EXT. RIVER CROSSING — CONTINUOUS

Gyða, unasked, is already sorting them into pairs by the bank — heaviest bag with steadiest legs, exactly the way you'd load a departure gate.

GYÐA

Sigríður with María. Hulda with me. Unnur, Súsanna, you're together anyway.

Nobody questions the order. It is, obviously, correct.

They go two at a time, arms linked, facing upstream. Stefanía positions herself mid-current, a fixed point, hands out for each pair as they pass.

María and Sigríður first. The water hits mid-thigh. María's boots fill instantly. She does not react.

MARÍA

(through it)

It's fine.

SIGRÍÐUR ÁSDÍS

Your lips are blue.

MARÍA

It's a controlled temperature.

Sigríður's foot goes. María has her by the arm before it registers — fast, certain, one movement — and holds her upright until she has the riverbed back. Lets go. Says nothing.

It took two seconds. Neither of them notices it happened.

Three steps further on, Stefanía, already braced mid-current, reaches out a hand.

STEFANÍA

María. Take my hand.

MARÍA

I've got better grip on the gravel.

Beat.

STEFANÍA

María.

MARÍA

If I take your hand, my balance calculation drops thirty percent.

Stefanía lowers her hand.

María crosses alone.

Hulda and Gyða next. Hulda, halfway across, stops.

HULDA

I know this river.

GYÐA

Keep moving.

HULDA

I've crossed this exact river before.

GYÐA

(pulling her)

Different water every time, Hulda. That's the whole thing about rivers.

Unnur and Súsanna last, before Rakel, who insists on going alone with the trolley, one wheel already spinning free in the current.

Everyone watches. The trolley goes in at an angle. For one full second, it looks like it might actually be swept downstream, taking Rakel with it.

It does not.

Rakel emerges on the far bank, drenched to the collar, trolley intact, wheels still spinning from momentum.

RAKEL

(triumphant, breathless)

Told you it rolls.

HULDA

It rolled sideways down a river.

RAKEL

It rolled.

EXT. FAR BANK — MOMENTS LATER

Eight soaked women, one dry trolley's worth of relief. Boots emptied one by one, water poured out in streams. Sigríður holds hers upside down for a full ten seconds.

Súsanna sits calmly on a rock, removing her boots with the unbothered air of someone arriving at a spa.

She peels off her socks. They are completely dry.

A silence spreads down the line as this is noticed, one woman at a time.

HULDA

How.

SÚSANNA

(already putting them back on)

Technique.

GYÐA

That's not an answer.

SÚSANNA

It's the only one you're getting.

Gyða opens her mouth to try one more angle. Unnur, without looking up from her boots:

UNNUR

Leave it.

GYÐA

I wasn't going to—

UNNUR

You were about to.

Gyða closes her mouth. Nobody in the group knows what she wasn't going to ask. That is, apparently, the point.

Nobody presses further. This will clearly outlive the episode.

Seven women are soaked to the waist.

Unnur sits on a rock. Her hair is dry.

SÚSANNA

Unnur.

UNNUR

Yes.

SÚSANNA

You crossed the same river we did.

UNNUR

Yes.

Silence.

SÚSANNA

How.

UNNUR

I crossed it.

Súsanna looks at her for a long time.

Unnur does not look back.
